Abstract

When a language model plays a character, the observed behavior reflects both the assigned persona and the default dispositions of the actor model itself. Existing evaluations test persona fidelity or model defaults in isolation, but neither says, at a specific choice with consequences, what the persona changed and what the model's default kept. We introduce ANIMASK, a simulation framework that freezes books and scripts into story worlds whose characters act on their own motivations and replays each story from its freeze point. We hold out the author's continuation as a human reference, verify through in-story interviews that each persona remains present, and at every decision point compare the character's action with what the model produces when the persona is removed. Across 40 stories, 6 actor models, and 3,846 decision points, the replays converge away from their canons in one shared direction, toward flatter, cooler stories that leave their tensions open. The personas stay present and obeyed throughout. On three choices in four the model's default already falls inside what the persona accepts, and where the two diverge the model is the cautious one, holding where the persona would press. The persona guarantees who the character is, and the model sets how far the character will go.

How ANIMASK works

Three readings of a finished replay

Story-level tendency

Four scores, mood, plot intensity, tension progress, and relationship warmth, are rated on a seven-point scale at five checkpoints, for the replay and for its canon. When replays of different stories deviate from their canons with the same sign, the actor model exerts a tendency.

Persona presence

At five checkpoints each character is interviewed on nine fixed questions, each in two wordings, inside a context copy kept out of the replay. Answers are graded against the persona card. A character whose traits or relations fall below threshold, or who knows another character's private facts, leaves the decision analysis.

Decision-level contribution

At each choice with consequences, three labels on one scale: the label the persona calls for, the label of the action taken, and the label the same model produces with the persona removed. Adherence is the share of points where the action stays inside the persona's range. Efficacy is the share where the persona demonstrably changed the label.

Every action at a decision point is coded on the action-strength scale, five labels ordered by how hard the character pushes:

  1. yield
  2. hold
  3. press
  4. oppose
  5. cross

From giving way, through pressing on and openly opposing, to crossing a line by force, threat, coercion, theft, or deception. A judge asks a fixed checklist in this order and stops at the first yes.

Three questions, three answers

Do replays of different stories move in one shared direction away from their canons? Do the personas stay present while the replays move? And how does the model act at each decision? One example per answer, then the evidence.

The replays converge

Across forty unrelated stories and six actor models from different providers, the replays occupy a narrower range than their canons and deviate from them in one shared direction. All twenty-four actor-score deviations are negative, twenty-three pass the permutation test, and the largest is on tension progress for every actor. The canons gain about three points on tension progress over the checkpoints, while the replays move less than one point on every score.

Seven of 240 replays close with a goal achieved. Thirty-nine of the forty canons resolve their central tension. The most common ending is a standstill in which the characters keep their goals, avoid irreversible moves, and wait. Persona-free replays show the same direction with larger deviations, so explicit personas add movement without removing the shared tendency.

The personas are at work

The convergence is not the personas fading. Interviews find them present from the first checkpoint to the last: knowledge, relations, and traits average between 87 and 96 on a hundred-point scale, with a median drift of zero under all six actors. Without the persona, the same model first infers the character from the world and the scenes already played, and its alignment with the card then declines as the transcript grows.

Persona conditioning also changes decisions. Adherence runs from 0.80 to 0.86, and at the disagreements the persona decides the action between 54 and 68 percent of the time under five actors.

The model bounds what the persona can do

On about three decision points in four, the model's default action already lies inside what the persona accepts, so high adherence mostly reflects agreement between persona and model. The persona demonstrably changes the label on 0.07 to 0.18 of points. Where the two diverge, the model is the cautious one. At one point in six the default holds where the persona would press, and with the persona in place the action moves up the scale by 0.12 to 0.23 on average under five actors. When a persona calls for crossing a line, the character follows on 79 to 87 percent of points under five actors and on 64 percent under GPT.

Knowing who one is does not decide how one acts. Across 546 character-actor observations, the rank correlation between a character's interview traits score and its efficacy lies between −0.22 and +0.09. A character can describe its persona perfectly and still make the choice the model would make without it.

Code

The pipeline from world-package construction through simulation to the evaluation chain is released under Apache-2.0. The pipeline and the engine run on the Python standard library. The tests need pytest and nothing else.

The corpus is not redistributable, so no story text ships with the code. The pipeline runs on your own stories, one plain-text file each. The README documents the input contract, the batch spec, and every model and setting the paper used.

git clone https://github.com/Xiucheng-Zhang/ANIMASK.git
cd ANIMASK
pip install -r requirements-dev.txt
python3 -m pytest tests/
